text: PAUL SZTORC’S BITCOIN HARD FORK COMES WITH REAL TAX IMPLICATIONS FOR BITCOINERS
  
  Longtime Bitcoin investor @jratcliff is raising concerns over tax risks for Bitcoin holders if August’s “eCash” fork gains traction.
  
  If a fork achieves wallet support, exchange listings, and real liquidity, it can create a taxable event under IRS Revenue Ruling 2019-24.
  
  Sztorc says you can “just ignore” the airdrop, but if you control your private keys, the IRS may treat newly created fork coins as ordinary income equal to their market value at the time you can access them, even if you never sell, claim, or use them.
  
  During the Bitcoin Cash split in 2017, holders were assigned taxable income based on fluctuating market prices, sometimes at elevated valuations they never realized in cash.
  
  The rule hinges on “dominion and control.” If you could access or dispose of the forked asset, the IRS may consider it income.
  
  That value becomes your cost basis, meaning if prices later collapse, you could face a mismatch between taxes owed and actual proceeds.
  
  In essence, a third party can effectively impose a tax liability on Bitcoin holders without their consent.
  
  Even choosing to ignore a fork does not necessarily eliminate exposure if access was possible.
  
  This is a clear example of how open-source network experimentation and rigid tax frameworks fail to fully account for the real-world impact of these events on users.
